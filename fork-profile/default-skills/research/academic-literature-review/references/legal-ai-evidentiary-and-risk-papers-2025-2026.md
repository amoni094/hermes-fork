## 1. Forensic chain-of-custody for AI-generated decision/recommendation logs

- **No real standard extends chain-of-custody methodology to AI/LLM-generated logs.** ISO/IEC 27043's only published version is **2015** — no 2026 revision, no "Annex C" on AI-generated logs exists (verified against iso.org's own OBP listing). NIST SP 800-86 is a pre-AI, general digital-forensics-integration guide with no AI-specific clause; a claimed "NIST IR 8497 (2025)" extending it to AI systems could not be verified against any primary NIST source (csrc.nist.gov). **Both were caught as fabrications inherited from a compacted-context summary** — see the citation-verification pitfall in the parent SKILL.md.
- **Current, real, and unfavorable to "forensic defensibility" claims**: Law & Forensics' "Digital Evidence in the Courtroom: 2026 Mid-Year Review" synthesizes four federal rulings (Feb–May 2026) showing courts are still split on basic AI-work-product privilege/discoverability questions — *Heppner* (S.D.N.Y.) found no privilege where a consumer AI platform's ToS permits training on submissions; *Warner v. Gilbarco* (E.D. Mich.) reached the opposite result ("tools, not persons"); *Morgan v. V2X* (D. Colo.) set out a disclosure/protective-order checklist; *CLF v. Shell* (D. Conn., Magistrate Farrish) held an expert's AI prompts discoverable as methodology under Rule 26. *White v. Walmart* (S.D. Ind.) sanctioned counsel for substituting AI output for independent analysis (Rule 26(g)). None of this validates any specific AI-decision-log design as admissible — it shows the underlying legal question is live and unsettled.
- **Best "recipe"-shaped practitioner source (vendor/consultancy, not peer-reviewed)**: LCG Discovery's "AI With Integrity" (Aug 2025) — five-pillar framework (FRE 901/902 authentication, chain-of-custody extended to data inputs/model artifacts/logs/human validation, Daubert/FRE 702 reliability, bias/hallucination auditing, litigation-readiness). Cites *Kohls v. Ellison* (fabricated-citation AI declaration excluded; $31k sanctions elsewhere). Useful for the *shape* of a defensible log (metadata stamping, encrypted immutable trail, periodic audits) but is marketing content with no judicially-tested claim of admissibility.
- **Adjacent, non-forensic anchor stat**: NHI Mgmt Group (June 2026) — only 5.7% of orgs have full visibility into service accounts; for agentic workloads, logs need to capture each decision point, not just the access event. Useful as a falsifiable technical floor (a log that only records login events fails even generic security-observability standards, before any admissibility question is reached).

## 2. Amendment-category extraction accuracy for contract redlines

- **"Amendment-category extraction" is not a recognized, benchmarked task** in the most current comprehensive survey of legal contract classification (Singh et al., UNSW, arXiv:2507.21108, Jul 2025) — 7 task types, 14 datasets reviewed, none matching this framing.
- **Closest real academic proxy — amendment *linkage* detection (binary, not categorical)**: Song (Hyperlex), RELATED Workshop @ ICAIL 2021 — 91% F1 on 1,124 real contract-amendment pairs (FR+EN), using engineered similarity features; 34% of remaining errors traced to upstream OCR/NER noise, not the classifier — a caution that end-to-end pipeline accuracy will be lower than clean-text benchmark accuracy.
- **CUAD / ContractEval (arXiv:2508.03080, Aug 2025, CMU/Rutgers/Stanford/NJIT)**: 19 LLMs benchmarked on CUAD's 4,128 data points / 41 clause categories; proprietary models beat open-source; open-source models show a "laziness" failure (false "no relevant clause"); **reasoning/"thinking" mode reduces correctness** on this span-extraction task; accuracy is non-uniform and drops on rarer/longer clause categories — argues against reporting a single blended accuracy number.
- **CLAUSE discrepancy benchmark (Findings of ACL: EACL 2026, ASU)**: 12,869+11,086 perturbations across CUAD/ContractNLI, 10 perturbation categories; LLM explanation-quality scores cluster 1.0–4.3/5; **consistently weakest on "omission"-type contradictions** (missing clauses) vs. explicit textual inconsistencies — directly relevant since amendment detection structurally requires detecting omissions/changes relative to a baseline, exactly the sub-task models are weakest on.
- **Vendor accuracy claims (Kira, Luminance) are unaudited marketing, not measured benchmarks** — see the vendor-marketing-claims pitfall in the parent SKILL.md. A public PR dispute between the two vendors (post-Litera acquisition) independently confirms these figures are adversarial marketing content, not neutral measurement. No independent peer-reviewed validation of either tool's accuracy was found.

## 3. Conformal prediction / calibrated confidence for legal risk-tier routing

- **No paper applies conformal prediction primarily to legal/compliance risk-tier routing** — this is a genuine, stated gap as of this sweep, not a search failure.
- **RouteNLP (arXiv:2604.23577, Apr 2026, HKU/Stellaris AI)** — closest and strongest finding. 8-week production pilot (~5,000 queries/day): conformal-cascade routing cut inference cost 58% at 91% response-acceptance, p99 latency 1,847ms→387ms. Six-task benchmark **explicitly includes "Legal Risk"**: 86.1% accuracy under conformal cascade vs. 88.3% always-largest-model ceiling (a 2.2-pt gap, the largest reported degradation among legal tasks). Critical caveat stated by the authors themselves: the conformal guarantee is **marginal, not per-query**, and **coverage-violation rate nearly doubles (4.2%→8.1%) under a tested 20% domain-shift scenario** — a concrete, quantified reason any legal risk-tier router needs ongoing calibration-drift monitoring, not a one-time calibration claim.
- **Cresswell et al., "Conformal Prediction Sets Improve Human Decision Making" (arXiv:2401.13744, Jan 2024)** — pre-registered RCT across 3 tasks: conformal sets improve human review accuracy vs. fixed top-k sets (p<0.05), most pronounced for "singleton" (high-confidence) sets. Not legal-domain-specific but directly supports the general design principle for a human-override-gate UI: show calibrated, variable-size uncertainty, not a flat confidence number.
- **Zhou, Orfanoudaki, Zhu, "CREDO" (arXiv:2505.13243, May 2025, CMU/Oxford)** — combines inverse-optimization + conformal prediction + generative modeling for a distribution-free bound on "this specific human-chosen decision is suboptimal." Explicitly acknowledges a curse-of-dimensionality/conservativeness tradeoff in the general (non-linear-optimization) case — caution against assuming conformal methods straightforwardly and tightly calibrate a multi-factor categorical risk-tier classifier.
- Two other on-topic sources (preprints.org "Coverage-Deferral Trade-Off," SSRN "Conformal Validation" deferral-policy paper) were blocked by anti-bot protection and were **flagged as unverified, not cited as findings** — follow the "flag paywalled/unverifiable inline" pitfall rather than presenting search-snippet-only sources with full-citation confidence.
