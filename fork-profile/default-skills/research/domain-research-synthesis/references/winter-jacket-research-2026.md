## Brief Relaxation Framework

When a user asks for a jacket with a strict spec, consider relaxing it step by step:

| Strict spec | Relaxed spec | What opens up |
|---|---|---|
| Windproof (100% barrier) | Wind resistant (high resistance) | Pertex Quantum, DWR nylons, all major down jackets |
| Waterproof (membrane) | Water resistant (DWR coat) | Removes need for Gore-Tex, saves weight and cost |
| Goose down | Duck down or synthetic | Broader mid-range options |
| Ripstop outer | Any durable nylon | More aesthetic options |

Down jackets with DWR coating handle light-to-moderate rain well. Gore-Tex is overkill
for most non-alpine use. Wind resistant is sufficient for urban/bushwalk use in AU.

## Down Jacket Construction Taxonomy

The #1 aesthetic question users have: "why do they look puffy and quilted?"

### Sewn-through baffles (most common, worst look)
- Outer and inner fabric stitched together at regular intervals
- Creates visible channel lines / sausage compartments on the exterior
- Cheap, light, effective insulation — but distinctive puffy look
- Examples: most budget-to-mid down jackets (Patagonia Down Sweater, Uniqlo Ultra Light)

### Structured / thick shell (hides baffles)
- Shell fabric is heavy/stiff enough to not pucker between baffle channels
- Baffles exist internally but are invisible externally
- Examples: Fjallraven Greenland No.1 (G-1000 shell), Canada Goose Langford (Arctic Tech)
- User reference: "the Fjallraven look" — flat, structured, not sportsy

### Box wall / internal baffle construction
- Outer and lining fabrics are NOT stitched together at baffle seams
- Creates internal boxes; completely smooth outer face
- Warmer than sewn-through (no cold spots at seam lines)
- Examples: Rab Neutrino Pro, Rab Mythic Ultra, Mountain Equipment Glacier
- Best technical choice if smooth exterior is required with proper down insulation

### Shell/liner construction
- Down is in a separate liner inside a clean nylon outer shell
- No visible quilting at all — looks like a regular jacket
- Examples: Arc'teryx Thorium (down inside clean nylon outer, no external baffle lines)
- Cleanest exterior look of all options

## Windproof vs Wind-Resistant

**Truly windproof** (physical barrier — waxed cotton, laminated membranes):
- Arc'teryx Alpha Parka (Gore-Tex Infinium)
- Canada Goose Langford (Arctic Tech)
- Fjallraven Greenland No.1 (G-1000 waxed cotton/poly)
- Uniqlo Seamless Down (windproof claim, DWR)
- Helikon-Tex Husky Tactical (Climashield Apex)
- Swazi Windriver (AEGIS Windshield fabric)
- Waxed cotton jackets generally (12.5oz+ canvas is a physical wind barrier)

**Highly wind-resistant (NOT windproof)**:
- Rab Neutrino Pro / Positron Pro (Pertex Quantum Pro — very high resistance, not a barrier)
- Most Pertex Quantum and Quantum Air shells
- DWR-treated ripstop nylon shells generally

For urban/bushwalk AU use, wind-resistant is usually sufficient unless exposed alpine/coastal.

## Wax Cotton + Wool Lining + Hood Category

Fundamentally different category from down. Trade-offs:

PROS:
- Physically windproof (no membrane needed — canvas is the barrier)
- Extremely durable: 12–14oz cotton canvas outlasts any nylon shell
- Gets better with age — rewax seasonally, lasts decades
- Wool lining stays warm even if outer soaks through
- No baffle seams = no cold-air leak paths

CONS:
- Heavy: 2–3x weight of equivalent down puffy
- Wool lining warm but not lofted — less warmth-to-weight than 800FP down
- Needs rewaxing (~10–15 min, once per season or when water stops beading)
- Stiff in cold until broken in
- Very few hooded options (most classic wax jackets predate hood fashion — designed for flat caps)

### Hood Caveat (Wax Cotton Category)
Most classic waxed jackets were designed without integrated hoods (traditional use: flat cap
or tweed hat). Hoods came later as accessories. If integrated hood is MANDATORY, options
narrow to: Wintergreen Northern Wear + boutique brands.
