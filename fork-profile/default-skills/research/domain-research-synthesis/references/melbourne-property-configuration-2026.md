# Melbourne Property Configuration Sweet Spot (2026)

Research date: July 2026. For PPOR buyer ~$1.1M budget in middle-ring Melbourne.

## Core principle

Configuration determines **buyer pool size at resale**. Maximise overlap between:
- Families upgrading from apartments (hard requirement: 4BR + 2 bath)
- Downsizers who still want space
- WFH professionals (home office = 4th bedroom non-negotiable post-COVID)
- Future FHBs as prices normalise

---

## Bedrooms

**Sweet spot: 4 bedrooms**

| Config | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| 3BR | Majority stock at this price. Smallest buyer pool. Acceptable ONLY if study/retreat can convert to 4BR with minimal work (wall + door). |
| 4BR | Sweet spot. Family + WFH dual demand. If area median 4BR is $1.3-1.5M, buying at $1.1M = embedded equity. |
| 5BR | Avoid. Usually compressed floorplan or poor extension. Narrows resale pool (large families only). |

JBK Homes data: adding a bedroom adds 8-15% value in family-demand suburbs — but only if floorplan is not compromised.

**Fallback**: 3BR + convertible study on 600m²+ NRZ-zoned block where rear extension is feasible.

---

## Bathrooms

**Sweet spot: 2 bathrooms (main + ensuite)**

| Config | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| 1 bathroom | Major objection from family buyers. Buyers discount MORE than actual reno cost (~$25-50k). Hard avoid. |
| 2 bath (main + ensuite) | Floor for family buyers. Maximises buyer pool. Target as minimum. |
| 3 bathrooms | Nice-to-have but rarely earns premium at $1.1M. Only adds value at $1.5M+ where expected. At $1.1M often signals space misuse. |

Powder room (separate toilet downstairs) = bonus livability, not counted as bathroom for marketing but valued by families.

Renovation return: main → ensuite step adds 5-8% to resale in family suburbs. Highest-returning bathroom investment.

---

## Car spaces

**Sweet spot: Double garage (side-by-side), lock-up**

| Config | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Street only | Active penalty: permit zones, school congestion, security concerns. Avoid in family suburbs. |
| Single LUG + 1 hardstand | Minimum acceptable. |
| Double garage (tandem) | Less valued than side-by-side. Tandem = negotiation opportunity, not a premium feature. |
| Double garage (side-by-side) | Sweet spot. Mandatory in car-dependent suburbs (65-70% car commute rate). |

ABS census data: Ringwood, Mitcham, Watsonia, Macleod, Montmorency all have 65-70%+ car commute rates. Double garage is functional infrastructure, not luxury.

**Note on inner suburbs**: If suburb is <8km from CBD on a tram line (cycle/walk feasible), single garage + street is more acceptable. For outer suburbs (>15km, high car dependency), double garage is close to non-negotiable.

---

## Land size

**Sweet spot: 550-700m²**

| Size | Assessment |
|------|-----------|
| <350m² | Feels cramped for 4BR family. No extension room. Avoid unless inner-ring with no alternatives. |
| 350-450m² | Minimum. Only acceptable if floorplan already optimised (no extension needed). |
| 550-700m² | Sweet spot. Maintains garden appeal, allows extension, some NRZ configs allow rear dwelling/granny flat. |
| >900m² | Subdivision potential becomes real but at $1.1M in these suburbs, large lots are rare exceptions. If available: premium worth paying. |

NRZ note: In Neighbourhood Residential Zone (McKinnon, Ormond, Bentleigh, Mitcham areas), 550m²+ can allow a small rear dwelling or granny flat. This adds rental income optionality and resale value. Check DELWP planning overlay for specific address.

## Commercial vs PPOR comparison (decision framework)

### When PPOR wins (almost always for this profile)
- At 47% marginal rate, PPOR CGT exemption worth ~$191K over 10 years, ~$594K over 20 years
- Shares need 9.15% p.a. return to match 7% p.a. PPOR return after-CGT
- PPOR mortgage: up to 80% LVR, 5.9-6.2% rate, no margin calls
- Commercial: 60-65% LVR, rates 1-1.5% higher, vacancy risk 6-18 months
- PPOR CGT exemption is unaffected by 2027 CGT reform

### When commercial makes sense
- As SECOND asset after PPOR established (5-7 years)
- Inside SMSF: 15% fund tax on income (vs 47% personal), 10% CGT on gains >12 months
- Post-2027: neg gearing reform applies to established residential investment but NOT commercial
- Current best commercial sector: industrial/logistics (strong post-COVID fundamentals)
- At $1.1M budget: only accesses bottom tier (strata retail/office) — weaker tenant quality,
  higher vacancy risk. Quality commercial is $2M+

### Commercial at $1.1M risks
- Small strata retail/office: higher business failure rate tenants
- Melbourne CBD office vacancy 18-20% (near historic highs, July 2026)
- E-commerce structurally suppressed strip retail
- GST complexity on purchase/sale
- Need larger deposit (35-40% vs 20% residential) for same LVR protection


**Lifestyle override**: Gap between ranks 1-4 and Murrumbeena/Cheltenham is ~$100-200k
over 10 years. A PPOR you exit early (due to commute friction) at year 5-6 loses stamp
duty absorption and CGT benefit. Lifestyle fit matters more than theoretical rank.

**Cheltenham vs Murrumbeena decision (July 2026)**:
- Murrumbeena thesis does NOT depend on McKinnon SC zone — core thesis is ABS-confirmed
  demographic undervaluation (nearly identical census profile to McKinnon/Ormond at $500-800k
  discount). School zone is additive upside only.
- Cheltenham wins overall for a 15-20yr holder: harder catalyst (SRL confirmed infra vs
  perception catch-up), higher separate house % (72% vs 64%), $1.1M more reliably buys
  4BR freestanding, larger demographic re-rating distance to travel (54% Prof+Mgr now,
  heading toward Bentleigh 61%/McKinnon 65%), possible Frankston HS zone (19.9% premium
  documented by REIV, verify per address at findmyschool.vic.gov.au).
- Murrumbeena wins for 10yr holder or if SRL timeline risk is a concern (2035 is 9 years;
  Victorian government fiscal pressure could delay further).
- Both are on Frankston line — inspect both simultaneously; let specific property quality
  and verified school zone status drive final decision.
