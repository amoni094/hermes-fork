# Negative Gearing & CGT Reform 2026 — Knowledge Bank

Law enacted: Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 +
Income Tax Rates Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026
Registered: 26 June 2026. Core changes effective: **1 July 2027**.

Sources: budget.gov.au, treasury.gov.au, CBA Senior Economist (13 May 2026),
Forge Property explainer (26 June 2026), Dingle Partners (Melbourne property guide).

---

## The Two Changes

### 1. Negative Gearing Restriction

From 1 July 2027, rental losses on **established residential investment properties**
cannot be offset against salary or wage income.

Instead: losses are **quarantined** — carried forward and used only against:
- Future residential rental income, OR
- Capital gains from residential property

**Exceptions (still fully negatively gearable):**
- New builds that add to housing supply
- Investments supporting government affordable housing programs
- Properties held before 7:30pm AEST 12 May 2026 (Budget night) — **GRANDFATHERED**
  until the property is sold
- Properties contracted (but not settled) before Budget night: also grandfathered

**Transition window:** Properties bought between Budget night and 30 June 2027 may
be negatively geared during that period, but quarantine rules apply from 1 July 2027.

**Lock-in effect (important for PPOR buyers):** Grandfathered investors face a strong
incentive NOT to sell — selling forfeits grandfathering permanently. This constrains
supply in desirable established suburbs even as demand softens.

**Cash-flow equivalent impact (CBA, May 2026):** Removing negative gearing is equivalent
to roughly a **90-155 basis point increase** in investor mortgage rates for affected properties.

### 2. CGT Discount Replacement

From 1 July 2027, the 50% CGT discount for individuals, trusts, and partnerships is
replaced for affected assets with:
- **Cost base indexation** (protect against inflation component)
- **30% minimum tax rate** on real capital gains

**Who is affected:** Investors in established residential property bought post-Budget.
New builds: can choose old 50% discount OR new indexation method, whichever is better.

**Transitional treatment:** For assets already owned before 1 July 2027, gains split:
- Gains accruing before 1 July 2027: old 50% discount rules apply
- Gains accruing after 1 July 2027: new indexation + 30% minimum tax applies
- Practical advice: obtain a valuation around 1 July 2027 to document the split

**When indexation is better vs worse than 50% discount (Treasury example):**
- $500K asset, 2.5% inflation, 10yr hold, 5%pa real growth
- Result: higher taxable gain under indexation than under 50% discount at high returns
- At low real returns + high inflation: indexation may reduce tax
- Rule of thumb: at high real capital growth (Melbourne historical ~6%pa), old 50%
  discount was better for investors. New rules INCREASE their tax burden.

**PPOR exemption: UNCHANGED**
- Section 118-110 ITAA 1997 remains intact
- PPOR owners pay $0 CGT regardless of gain, holding period, or marginal rate
- This is the biggest advantage of PPOR vs investment property and it widened post-reform

## Other Budget Housing Measures

- $2bn Local Infrastructure Fund (last-mile infrastructure for new housing)
- Foreign purchase ban on established dwellings: extended to 30 June 2029
- Build-to-rent: strengthened affordability requirements for BTR tax concessions
- Social and affordable housing: additional Commonwealth funding

## Market Price Impact

| Source | Estimated Impact on Established Dwelling Prices |
|--------|--------------------------------------------------|
| CBA (May 2026) | ~3% lower than they otherwise would have been |
| AMP / Shane Oliver | -5% nationally over 2026-27 |
| Domain FY27 forecast (Jun 2026) | Melbourne houses -4% to -8% FY27 |
| ANZ (Aug 2026 revision) | -4.3% (2026), -3.4% (2027); peak-to-trough -10.6% |

Note: most of these declines are also driven by 3 RBA rate rises in 2026 (Feb/Mar/May)
at 4.35% cash rate. Difficult to separate tax effect from rate effect. CBA estimated the
policy-specific impact at ~3%.

**Investor vs OO buyer dynamics post-Budget:**
- Investor demand for established properties reduced (fewer competing bidders)
- OO buyers (PPOR) are unaffected by NG/CGT changes and now have less competition
- New build demand from investors is structurally supported (exempted from changes)
- Rental market: some reduction in rental supply as marginal investors exit; rents forecast
  to rise 20% over 2-3 years in tightest suburbs (Australian Broker, 2026)

## Decision Framework for PPOR Buyer (Post-Reform)

1. **You are the preferred buyer class.** PPOR CGT exemption untouched; investor demand reduced;
   negotiating room increased. The reform structurally favoured PPOR buyers over investors.

2. **Do not rush.** Grandfathering means existing investor-sellers have LESS reason to sell
   (they lose the tax protection permanently). Supply in premium established suburbs may actually
   tighten despite softer prices. This is a nuanced dynamic: don't assume "investors are fleeing
   and stock will flood the market." The opposite is more likely in good suburbs.

3. **New builds:** If considering new construction, investor demand for new builds was
   PRESERVED by the exemption. This may push up new build prices relative to established.
   For PPOR purposes, established houses at negotiated discounts remain better value.

4. **Tax timing:** PPOR CGT exemption applies from day 1 of owner-occupation. The reform
   date (1 July 2027) is irrelevant for PPOR buyers — no transition needed, no valuation
   required, no split-gain calculation. Simplest tax outcome of any property type.
