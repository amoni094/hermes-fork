## NORTH: Suburb Profiles

### Macleod (3085) ★★★★★ — Best north pick

- **Clearance 88%** in a 54-60% metro market = strongest demand-scarcity signal in analysis
- PropTrack annual growth +18.74% (houses) — extraordinary outperformance
- 5yr growth ~31% (REIV Q2 2025 data, Collings GeeVee)
- Collings GeeVee score: **7.7/10** (higher than Watsonia at 7.5)
- Small suburb (~9,892 pop), 73% owner-occupier — tightly held, low supply
- **School:** Macleod College P-12 on-site; proximity to La Trobe Uni + Austin Hospital
- **Infrastructure:** Primary North East Link beneficiary — improved freeway access, 5 MCGs of new
  parkland along Banyule Creek, Greensborough Road boulevard. NEL opening ~2028.
- Springthorpe Estate premium pocket (country club precinct) — raises suburb ceiling
- All-residential median ~$1M; house-only median ~$1.2M
- Budget fit: Entry houses $950k-$1.15M available. Top of $900-$1.3M budget.

### Watsonia (3087) ★★★★ — Best budget north-east pick

- **NEL tunnel portal** — 6.5km tunnel starts here (opens ~2028)
- After NEL: 15,000 trucks/day removed from local roads = major amenity improvement
- Hurstbridge Line tunnel between Watsonia–Greensborough extended (3rd longest in VIC)
- Small suburb (5,352 pop), 68% OO — limited supply
- **School:** Loyola College (highly regarded Catholic secondary) = quasi-school-zone appeal
- Collings GeeVee score: **7.5/10** (REIV Q2 2025 data)
- 5yr growth ~28%; annual +9.1% (outperforming market in downturn)
- All-res median ~$900k; house-only ~$1.0M
- Budget fit: Most houses $900k-$1.05M — **best budget fit on Hurstbridge line**

### Reservoir (3073) ★★★★ — Best value / gentrification spillover

- Melbourne's largest suburb by area — highly heterogeneous (different pockets vary widely)
- **4 train stations** (Reservoir, Regent, Keon Park, Ruthven) + 2 tram lines
- Annual growth +5.56% (houses); clearance 58% (above metro average)
- Priced ~$250k below Thornbury, ~$200-250k below Coburg for equivalent freestanding house
- Broadway/Reservoir station precinct: consistent development activity
- Preston/Coburg gentrification spillover beneficiary — younger professional demographic moving in
- Best pocket: south of Broadway near Thornbury border + Edwardes Lake precinct
- Avoid: main road frontage (Murray Rd, Spring St)
- Budget fit: $850k-$1.0M for freestanding house — **easiest budget fit north**

### Montmorency (3094) ★★★ — Village feel, stable

- 81% owner-occupier rate (one of Melbourne's highest) — extremely stable
- 71% clearance rate in a 54-60% market (strong demand signal)
- Were Street village: artisan cafes, bakeries, boutique shops — genuine community feel
- Montmorency Secondary College: well-regarded local secondary
- Plenty River corridor: leafy walking trails; distinctive hilly terrain
- All-res median ~$994k; house-only ~$1.2M
- Budget fit: Entry stock at $950k-$1.15M — just within budget at lower end
- Compare: ~$200k cheaper than Eltham (premium Hurstbridge suburb) with same line

### Greensborough (3088) — Notable mention

- 75% clearance rate in soft market — demand signal
- Families suburb: 77% families, Greensborough Plaza (Hoyts cinema, major retail)
- NEL benefit: new 2km tree-lined boulevard on Greensborough Road
- 80% owner-occupier rate (very stable)
- Budget fit: Entry houses $850k-$1.0M — fits budget well
- Lower growth story than Macleod/Watsonia but more accessible price

### Coburg (3058) — Largely re-priced

- 8km from CBD, Upfield Line + multiple trams
- 6.55% annual growth; strong gentrification story (Pentridge Prison redevelopment: 17-ha urban village)
- "The Grove" and Elizabeth St prestige pockets
- Budget: Freestanding houses mostly $1.0M-$1.4M — borderline/above budget
- Window may be closing. Better value in Preston/Reservoir.

### Preston (3072) — Reasonable but clearance weak

- Multiple train stations (Preston, Bell) on Mernda line + Route 86 tram
- High Street cafe/dining transformation
- Annual growth +3.45%; clearance **48%** (below metro average — weakness signal)
- House-only median ~$1.2M; entry stock $1.0M-$1.15M
- Budget: Fits but clearance weakness = market soft here

### Thornbury (3071) — ABOVE BUDGET

- Median house ~$1.5M. 6.87% growth, 66% clearance. Out of $900k-$1.3M range.

## EAST: Suburb Profiles

### Ringwood (3134) ★★★★★ — Best east pick / infrastructure-led value

- **Cheltenham analogy**: infrastructure complete/underway, prices not yet fully repriced
- $665M Eastland redevelopment (world-class retail/outdoor precinct)
- Level crossing removal on BOTH Belgrave and Lilydale lines COMPLETE
- Melbourne's Transit City designation — 10-20yr structural demand driver
- Major interchange: Belgrave Line + Lilydale Line (2 direct CBD routes)
- EastLink freeway junction (exceptional vehicle access)
- Realm: landmark library/community hub
- Ringwood Secondary College: respected public secondary (performing arts focus)
- Annual growth +3.07%; vacancy **1.3%** (extremely tight)
- Collings GeeVee: 7.6/10 with Infrastructure score 8.5/10 (highest in east analysis)
- 5yr annualised growth 5.8%
- All-res median ~$841k; house-only ~$920k-$1.0M
- Budget fit: $900k-$1.1M for freestanding house — **best budget fit in east**
- Note: Clearance 33% for auction (low); but overall 50% incl private sales — normal market

### Mitcham (3132) ★★★★ — Character suburb with two train stations

- **Two train lines**: Mitcham station (Lilydale/Belgrave) + Antonio Park station
- Annual growth +5.93% in downturn; 5yr ~30%
- 58% clearance (above metro average)
- 71% owner-occupier rate (stable, family suburb)
- 23 local parks including Yarran Dheran Nature Reserve
- Schwerkolt Cottage (1880s heritage; world's first feature film filmed here 1906)
- Active mid-century home renovation/rebuild cycle
- Compare: ~$300-400k cheaper than Blackburn ($1.6M) for comparable leafy feel
- Budget fit: Entry stock $1.0-$1.2M. Median $1.3M at top of budget.
- Two train lines = flexibility and redundancy (Belgrave or Lilydale to city)

### Nunawading (3131) ★★★★ — Best clearance signal east / school-zone adjacent

- **77% clearance rate** in a 54-60% metro market = strongest demand signal in east analysis
- Annual growth +4.25%; 5yr ~23%
- Post-war 1950s homes on generous blocks; active medium-density development near station
- Whitehorse Road 'Mega Mile' commercial strip (major retail anchor)
- 67% owner-occupier; family suburb (70% families)
- Near EDSC zone boundary: out-of-zone Doncaster East ($1.47M) being priced out; families landing
  in nearby Nunawading. Check findmyschool.vic.gov.au for specific address zone status.
- Budget fit: Entry stock $950k-$1.15M. Median ~$1.2M. Fits budget at lower end.

### Croydon (3136) ★★★ — Best annual growth + best budget fit east

- **+9.15% annual growth** — strongest annual growth in entire east analysis
- Lilydale Line (direct CBD); close to Eastland, Chirnside Park, Westfield Knox
- Main Street: arts cinema, boutique cafes, village feel — differentiates from bland outer ring
- 70% owner-occupier; 12yr avg hold (loyalty signal)
- Budget fit: Most freestanding houses $850k-$1.0M — **easiest east budget fit**
- Trade-off: 32km from CBD (furthest in this analysis)
- Melba Secondary College (local public secondary — not elite)
- Luther College: P-12 private option

### Doncaster East (3109) — ABOVE BUDGET

- EDSC in-zone median house: **$1,667,500** (REIV Mar 2025) — above budget
- Out-of-zone median: $1,471,000 — also above budget for freestanding house
- EDSC zone premium: +13.4% for houses vs 1km outside (+$196k)
- No direct rail (buses to Box Hill station)
- Budget verdict: **Out of $900k-$1.3M range** for freestanding house

### Blackburn (3130) — ABOVE BUDGET

- Median house ~$1.6M. Leafy village, Blackburn Lake Sanctuary.
- Annual growth +5.67%; clearance 52%.
- Highly regarded Blackburn High School (performing arts/music).
- Blackburn + Laburnum stations (Belgrave/Lilydale).
- **Out of budget** for freestanding house.

### Box Hill South (3128) — ABOVE BUDGET (and weak demand)

- Median house ~$1.5M.
- Annual growth only +2.56%; clearance **41%** (weak — below metro average).
- Partial Balwyn High School catchment (check findmyschool.vic.gov.au for address).
- Balwyn HS zone median (Balwyn North): $2.23M — far outside budget.
- **Out of budget** for typical freestanding house; odd entry stock may hit $1.1-$1.3M.

**Key corrective note:** Brentwood SC is in **Glen Waverley (3150)**, not near Mitcham or Nunawading.
Many suburb analyses conflate Mitcham/Nunawading/Ringwood with Brentwood SC zone — they are NOT in it.
Brentwood SC is adjacent to Glen Waverley SC zone (also 3150).

For families requiring Balwyn HS or EDSC access on a $900k-$1.3M budget:
- No freestanding house option exists inside either zone at that budget
- Best approximation: Nunawading addresses near Doncaster East boundary (some may fall in EDSC zone)
- Always verify: findmyschool.vic.gov.au for the specific address

## Infrastructure Catalyst: North East Link (NEL)

**Opening:** ~2028 (TBM Zelda broke through in Bulleen, May 2026)

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Tunnel | 6.5km twin tunnel, Watsonia → Bulleen |
| Traffic removed | 15,000 trucks/day off local roads |
| Travel time saving | Up to 35 minutes |
| Parkland created | 5 MCGs of new green space (Banyule Creek / Borlase Reserve) |
| Greensborough Rd | New 2km tree-lined boulevard |
| Yarra Link | 2-hectare green bridge over Bulleen Rd |
| Wetlands | 3 new wetlands (Bulleen/Balwyn North) |
| Hurstbridge Line | Extended tunnel Watsonia–Greensborough (now 3rd longest VIC) |

**Primary beneficiaries:** Watsonia (tunnel portal), Macleod (reduced congestion + freeway access),
Greensborough (boulevard upgrade), Rosanna, Bulleen  
**Secondary beneficiaries:** Doncaster, Templestowe (reduced truck traffic), Heidelberg, Ivanhoe

**Property thesis:** NEL not fully priced into Macleod/Watsonia prices as of July 2026.
Window narrowing as 2028 completion approaches. Compare to: Level crossing removals already
reflected in Ringwood prices (complete), SRL East priced into Glen Waverley etc.

## Key Analytical Framework for North/East Melbourne

### Clearance rate as scarcity signal in downturn
In a market with 54-60% metro clearance, abnormally HIGH clearance signals genuine scarcity
(not hype). Hierarchy from this research:
- Macleod 88% → exceptional scarcity
- Nunawading 77% → strong scarcity
- Greensborough 75% → solid scarcity
- Montmorency 71% → above average
- Thornbury 66% → above average (but >median price)
- 58-60%: Preston, Coburg, Mitcham — market average
- Below average: Box Hill South 41%, Ringwood auction 33% (but total 50%), Preston 48%

### Gentrification wave pattern (north)
Brunswick (complete) → Coburg (complete/repriced) → **Preston** (mid) → **Reservoir** (early/mid)
→ Fawkner/Glenroy (early)
Budget sweet spot: Reservoir (leading edge of wave; $200k discount to Preston; not yet re-priced)

### Price gap analysis — north (vs Thornbury benchmark at $1.5M)
- Thornbury: $1.5M (inner north benchmark)
- Coburg: $1.2M (-$300k, 8km, comparable fundamentals)
- Preston: $1.2M (-$300k, 9-10km, comparable transport)
- Reservoir: $950k (-$550k, 12km, multiple trains) ← biggest relative discount
- Macleod: $1.2M (-$300k, 13km, NEL catalyst, 88% clearance)
- Watsonia: $1.0M (-$500k, 16km, NEL portal)

### Price gap analysis — east (vs Blackburn benchmark at $1.6M)
- Blackburn: $1.6M (east benchmark)
- Mitcham: $1.3M (-$300k, comparable leafy feel + two train stations)
- Nunawading: $1.2M (-$400k, same train lines, 77% clearance)
- Ringwood: $920k (-$680k, Transit City, Eastland, interchange) ← biggest relative discount
- Croydon: $966k (-$634k, 32km but +9.15% growth momentum)
