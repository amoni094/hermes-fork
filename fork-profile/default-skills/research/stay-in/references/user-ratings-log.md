## Critical distinction — protagonist anchor vs ensemble/system

### WORKS: one person in a crucible
The protagonist is trapped, under pressure, with something real at stake. You feel FOR them or FEAR FOR them.
- Whiplash: Andrew vs Fletcher. Andor: Cassian's survival. Slow Horses: Lamb holding his broken unit together. Rome: Vorenus's honour destroyed piece by piece.

### MISS: ensemble or system as hero
Even at high prestige, when the show distributes weight across 4+ equally weighted characters with no single dominant emotional anchor, it lands as "ok" not "loved".
- Succession 7/10: Roy family is fascinating but no single figure carries the weight.
- The Wire 8/10: institutional system portrait — respected, not loved.
- Mindhunter 6/10: the FBI profiling method is the protagonist, not Holden Ford.

### FILTER: before recommending any ensemble or procedural, ask:
1. Is there ONE figure whose fate you follow through the whole series/film?
2. Does that figure have something personal at stake beyond the case/mission?
3. Does the world have its own coherent logic the protagonist must navigate?
If yes to all 3: strong candidate. If no to 1 or 2: flag as risky.
