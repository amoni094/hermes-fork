# Philosophy / Intellectual History Synthesis — Adversarial Attack Vectors
## Added: August 2026 (Vervaeke meaning crisis synthesis session)

Use this checklist when performing adversarial review on a synthesis of a philosopher's
or intellectual historian's work — lectures, books, or multi-source argument reconstruction.
These vectors are distinct from scientific-paper and code review vectors; apply them in
addition to, not instead of, the standard research/knowledge-corpus vectors.

---

## Attack Vector 1: Genealogy-as-Causation Conflation

**The error:** Treating an intellectual genealogy (A led to B which enabled C) as a
causal mechanism equivalent to empirical causation. Nietzsche's genealogical method,
Foucault's genealogy, and Vervaeke's historical arc all trace structural logic, not
efficient causation. Presenting them as causal claims overstates their explanatory force.

**What to check:**
- Does the synthesis claim that Ockham's nominalism "caused" the Scientific Revolution,
  or that Luther "caused" narcissism? Downgrade to: "created conditions for" or
  "removed structural barriers to."
- Is the intellectual history compatible with materialist causal accounts (urbanization,
  inequality, kinship collapse, media technology)? If so, the intellectual account is
  complementary, not exclusive.
- Verdict language: "genealogy confirmed, causal mechanism unverified."

---

## Attack Vector 2: Romanticization of Premodern Life (Continuous Cosmos Myth)

**The error:** Reconstructing a premodern worldview as a coherent, harmonious whole
(Barfield's "Original Participation," Vervaeke's "continuous cosmos") without equally
representing the terror, violence, arbitrary death, social domination, and existential
suffering that were also features of that world.

**What to check:**
- Does the synthesis describe shamanic or pre-Axial life primarily in terms of what was
  gained (participatory knowing, flow, meaning) without noting what was lost or never
  present (physical security, equality, individual autonomy)?
- Is Barfield's "Original Participation" treated as empirically documented consciousness
  history or as a theoretical-poetic reconstruction? It is the latter; flag if presented
  as the former.
- Is the "premodern had it better on this axis" claim tested against historical
  anthropology, or just asserted from philosophical premises?
- Verdict: "romanticization risk — hold at one remove; requires independent
  anthropological corroboration."

---

## Attack Vector 3: Source-Authority Laundering

**The error:** A philosopher cites a contested or fringe source (Jung, Corbin, Barfield)
with the same authoritative weight as well-evidenced claims, and the synthesis inherits
this without flagging the differential epistemic status.

**What to check:**
- Jung is contested in academic psychology (archetypes lack empirical grounding; the
  collective unconscious is not a scientific claim). Does the synthesis treat Jung's
  claims about individuation and archetypes as scientifically grounded or as
  phenomenologically useful heuristics?
- Corbin's imaginal realm is a mystical-philosophical claim, not a cognitive-scientific
  one. Does the synthesis mark this as a philosophical/phenomenological contribution,
  not an empirical finding?
- Barfield's consciousness history has no empirical foundation beyond textual
  interpretation. Flag whenever it's presented as historical fact.
- Rule: each source should be cited with its epistemic tier: empirical claim /
  philosophical argument / phenomenological heuristic / speculative reconstruction.

---

## Attack Vector 4: Coherence-vs-Correspondence Conflation

**The error:** A synthesis that is internally coherent (all pieces fit together elegantly)
is taken as evidence of correctness about the world. High-quality philosophical synthesis
is seductive precisely because it achieves coherence — this is a feature of the synthesis,
not a test of the thesis.

**What to check:**
- Does the synthesis's coherence give it the appearance of being more established than it
  is? Note explicitly: "the argument is coherent; its truth is a separate question."
- Are there empirical predictions that follow from the thesis that could in principle be
  falsified? If not, the framework is explanatorily powerful but not scientifically
  testable — which is fine, but must be stated.
- Vervaeke's RR framework is a case study: it is a theoretically elegant convergence
  argument but does not generate falsifiable predictions beyond what existing cognitive
  science already predicts. Coherence ≠ confirmation.

---

## Attack Vector 5: Institutional-Feasibility Gap

**The error:** A constructive proposal is evaluated purely on its intellectual coherence
without examining whether it is institutionally feasible, socially scalable, or
historically novel in its requirements.

**What to check:**
- Vervaeke's "religion that is not a religion" requires: decentralized bottom-up structure,
  open-ended mythos, comprehensive psychotechnology ecology, wisdom framing, no creedal
  requirement, sustained over years. Has any prior attempt at this succeeded at scale?
  What failed and why?
- Does the proposal require an elite contemplative culture (long-term meditation retreat
  access, Socratic dialogue partners, communitas communities) that is unavailable to most
  people under material constraint (precarity, time poverty, social isolation)?
- Is the "ecology of psychotechnologies" grounded in evidence for each component, or
  primarily theoretically motivated? (Mindfulness replication crisis; communitas/Circling
  has very limited RCT evidence; higher states via psychedelics are promising but heavily
  regulated.)
- Verdict: "institutionally underspecified — intellectually coherent proposal with no
  validated delivery mechanism at scale."

## Worked Example: Vervaeke's Awakening from the Meaning Crisis (Aug 2026)

|--------|---------|----------|---------|
| Genealogy-as-causation | Historical arc presented as structural logic, not efficient causation; Vervaeke is explicit about this | LOW | Confirmed as honest; synthesis should make explicit |
| Continuous cosmos romanticization | Premodern suffering is under-represented; Barfield's Original Participation is not empirically grounded | MEDIUM | Partially confirmed; hold at one remove |
| Source-authority laundering | Jung and Corbin cited alongside cognitive neuroscience without epistemic tier marking | MEDIUM | Confirmed; flag in synthesis as "philosophical heuristic, not empirical claim" |

**Meta-verdict:** The synthesis survives adversarial review with its core structure
intact. Three attacks land at MEDIUM or below; one (institutional feasibility) lands
HIGH but Vervaeke partially acknowledges it. The framework is philosophically
sophisticated and convergently argued; it is not empirically established and should
not be presented as such.
