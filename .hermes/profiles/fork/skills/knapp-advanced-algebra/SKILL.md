## Overview

Knapp's Advanced Algebra (2007) is a rigorous graduate-level reference covering commutative algebra, homological algebra, algebraic number theory, and representation theory of finite groups. Presupposes knowledge of basic algebra. Companion to Basic Algebra.

## Structure / Chapters

- Chapter 1: Transition to Modern Number Theory
- Chapter 2: Wedderburn-Artin Ring Theory
- Chapter 3: Brauer Group
- Chapter 4: Homological Algebra
- Chapter 5: Three Theorems in Algebraic Number Theory
- Chapter 6: Reinterpretation with Adeles and Ideles
- Chapter 7: Infinite Field Extensions
- Chapter 8: Background for Algebraic Geometry
- Chapter 9: The Number Theory of Algebraic Curves
- Chapter 10: Methods of Algebraic Geometry

## Key Techniques

- Wedderburn structure theorem for semisimple algebras
- Ext and Tor functors in homological algebra
- Class field theory via ideles and adeles
- Galois cohomology and Brauer group computation
- Divisors and Riemann-Roch theorem for curves

## Anti-patterns

- Applying finite-dimensional results to infinite-dimensional settings without check
- Conflating the abstract Brauer group with its concrete matrix algebra representatives
- Using homological exact sequences without verifying flatness/projectivity conditions

## Anti-patterns

- Skipping foundational chapters when prerequisites are assumed
- Applying results outside their domain of validity (check assumptions)
- Confusing syntactic and semantic levels in formal treatments
